## 2.1 The 60-Second Self-Introduction

> **Rule**: Memorize the STRUCTURE, not the exact words. The delivery should feel natural, not rehearsed.

### Structure Framework

```
[10 sec] WHO YOU ARE
    → Name, degree, college, batch year

[15 sec] CORE IDENTITY — ANCHOR ON DATA
    → "My primary domain is data analytics"
    → SQL depth (window functions, CTEs, complex JOINs)
    → Python + Pandas for data manipulation

[15 sec] EXPERIENCE — BRIEF, HONEST
    → NullClass internship (Power BI dashboards)
    → Celebal Technologies (data engineering exposure)
    → Teleperformance (brief mention — communication skills)

[10 sec] FLAGSHIP PROJECT — DROP IT CONFIDENTLY
    → Crime Data Analytics Pipeline
    → 8 million records, Python + MySQL + Power BI
    → "I can walk you through any part of it"

[10 sec] WHY TCS — CLOSE STRONG
    → Scale of projects
    → Data/analytics alignment
    → Learning-oriented culture
```

### Scripted Version (Adapt, Don't Memorize Verbatim)

> "I'm Krishna Jodha, a B.Tech IT graduate from Poornima College of Engineering, Jaipur — 2025 batch.
>
> My primary domain is **data analytics**. I'm strongest in SQL — I work with window functions, CTEs, and complex multi-table joins regularly — and in Python with Pandas for large-scale data manipulation.
>
> During college, I completed a **data analyst internship at NullClass**, where I built Power BI dashboards analyzing social media engagement data. I also did a **data engineering virtual internship at Celebal Technologies** for exposure to pipeline architecture. After graduation, I worked briefly at **Teleperformance** in a customer-facing role, which sharpened my communication and high-pressure problem-solving.
>
> My most significant project is a **crime data analytics pipeline** built on 8 million records from the LA Open Data Portal — using Python, MySQL, and Power BI. I designed the ETL pipeline end-to-end, from chunked data ingestion to interactive dashboard delivery. I can walk you through any part of it.
>
> I'm excited about TCS because of the **scale and diversity of data projects** here. My SQL and Python foundation makes me a strong fit for data or analytics-aligned roles, and I see TCS as a place that genuinely invests in technical growth."

## 2.2 Explaining Teleperformance — The Bridge Role

> **Strategy**: Include it, don't hide it. Hiding creates a suspicious 5-month gap. Framing it correctly turns it from a weakness into a strength signal.

### If Asked: "What did you do after graduation?"

> "After graduating in May 2025, I joined **Teleperformance as a Customer Care Executive** while I continued searching for the right technical role. I took this position to stay financially active and to experience enterprise-scale customer operations firsthand — it genuinely improved my communication skills and my ability to handle pressure.
>
> However, the shift structure — often 10-hour rotating shifts — made it practically impossible to prepare for technical interviews in parallel. I made a **conscious, calculated decision** to resign in October 2025 and invest 100% of my time into technical upskilling. Clearing the TCS NQT Digital band is the direct outcome of that decision."

### Why This Framing Works

| Element | Signal It Sends |
|---------|----------------|
| "Financially active" | Responsible, not sitting idle |
| "Enterprise-scale operations" | Learning mindset, even in non-tech roles |
| "Conscious decision to resign" | Strategic thinking, not impulsive quitting |
| "Invest 100% in upskilling" | Committed to technical career |
| "Direct outcome" | Connects past decision to present success |

### What NOT to Say

- ❌ "I couldn't find a technical job so I had to take it" — Sounds desperate
- ❌ "It was a bad experience" — Badmouthing employers is a red flag
- ❌ "The pay was too low" — Makes you seem money-driven
- ❌ "I was not using my skills" — Sounds entitled

## 2.3 Explaining the Gap (October 2025 – March 2026)

### Direct Question: "What have you been doing since October?"

> "Since October 2025, I've been in a **dedicated technical preparation phase**. I was rejected in the DSA round at IBM in February 2026 — that was a genuine wake-up call. I realized that surface-level knowledge of algorithms wasn't enough for corporate recruitment.
>
> I took it seriously: built a structured study plan, practiced coding problems daily, deepened my SQL and Python skills with real projects, and prepared systematically for the TCS NQT. **Clearing the Digital band is the direct result of that focused effort.**
>
> I also used this time to refine my Crime Data Analytics project — improving the data cleaning pipeline and adding more sophisticated SQL queries."

### Why Mentioning IBM Rejection is POWERFUL

Most candidates hide rejections. Mentioning it shows:

1. **Self-awareness** — You know where you fell short
2. **Growth mindset** — You treated failure as data, not defeat
3. **Resilience** — You didn't give up; you course-corrected
4. **Authenticity** — Interviewers respect honesty over fabrication

## 2.4 Resume Timeline — The Clean Narrative

| Period | Role | How to Present |
|--------|------|---------------|
| Nov 2021 – May 2025 | B.Tech IT, Poornima College | CGPA 7.26 — meets TCS eligibility criteria |
| Jul 2024 – Aug 2024 | Data Analyst Intern, NullClass | Remote, 1 month. Power BI dashboards on social media data (10k+ records) |
| May 2025 – Jul 2025 | Data Engineering Intern, Celebal Technologies | Virtual. Exposure to PySpark, Delta Lake concepts. Capstone project was guided/scaffolded |
| May 2025 – Oct 2025 | Customer Care Executive, Teleperformance | Bridge role. Communication + pressure handling. Strategic resignation for tech focus |
| Oct 2025 – Present | Technical Preparation | Structured DSA/SQL/Python upskilling. NQT cleared (Digital band) |

### Resume Modifications BEFORE Interview

> **CRITICAL**: Make these changes before the interview day:

1. **REMOVE or DOWNGRADE**: "Deployed on AWS EC2" → Change to "Explored cloud deployment options"
2. **REMOVE**: Any mention of "cron jobs" or scheduled automation you didn't implement
3. **REMOVE**: Machine Learning implementation claims — your real pipeline doesn't use ML in the interview version
4. **ADD**: Teleperformance role (May–Oct 2025) — don't hide it
5. **SOFTEN**: Celebal capstone language — "Contributed to a guided capstone project" rather than "Built a complete system"

## 2.5 Handling Tricky Narrative Questions

### Q: "Why didn't you get placed through campus?"

> "Our college placement season had limited opportunities in data analytics — most on-campus companies were hiring for development or testing roles. I wanted to pursue my strength in data analytics, so I continued looking off-campus. In hindsight, I should have been more flexible earlier, but this journey has made my preparation far stronger."

### Q: "You seem overqualified for a fresher role. Why not a mid-level position?"

> "I appreciate that — but I believe in building a strong foundation. I'm confident in my SQL and Python skills, but I also know there's enormous depth to gain in enterprise-scale systems, which TCS offers. I'd rather start at the right level and grow rapidly than overstate my readiness."

### Q: "Do you use AI tools like ChatGPT for coding?"

> "Yes, I do — and I think it would be dishonest to claim otherwise in 2026. I use AI tools for **boilerplate code generation and syntax reference**. But I always audit the output for correctness, edge cases, and schema fit. For my Crime Data pipeline, the core logic — chunked processing, cleaning rules, SQL query design — is entirely my own. I can explain every line and every design decision."

### Q: "What's one thing you'd have done differently in your career so far?"

> "I would have committed to a single domain earlier. During college, I explored web development, data analysis, and data engineering in quick succession. If I had focused exclusively on data analytics from year 2, I'd have deeper expertise by now. That realization is exactly why I'm fully committed to this path going forward."

## 2.6 Body Language & Delivery Tips

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Maintain eye contact (60-70% of the time) | Stare at the table or your hands |
| Sit upright, lean slightly forward | Slouch or lean back |
| Use hand gestures naturally | Keep hands hidden under the table |
| Pause before answering difficult questions | Rush into answers immediately |
| Smile when greeting and thanking | Have a stone-face throughout |
| Say "That's a great question" when buying time | Say "I don't know" without any follow-up |
| Speak at a measured pace | Speak too fast (nervousness signal) |
